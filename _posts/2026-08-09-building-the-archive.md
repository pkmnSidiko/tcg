<h2>
    Building the Archive
</h2>

<p class="journal-date">
    August 9, 2026
</p>

<p>
    The new Sidiko TCG Archive is taking shape! I'm moving my collection
    projects, binder systems, methodology, and field notes into a proper
    Jekyll site.
</p>

<!--more-->

<p>
    I've been looking for a better way to share what I'm doing
    with my Pokémon TCG collection.
</p>

<p>
    Not just a list of cards I own, but the actual projects
    behind the collection: the binders, the rules I use,
    the things I'm researching, the cards I'm hunting for,
    and the occasional project that makes me wonder why I
    decided cardboard needed an engineering department.
</p>

<h3>
    Why Notion?
</h3>

<p>
    Notion is still doing the heavy lifting for the collection
    itself. It works very well as a database for wishlists,
    trades, collection status, master-set tracking, artist
    collections, and my generational Pokédex projects.
</p>

<p>
    What it doesn't do particularly well is feel like a journal.
    I want somewhere I can write an actual update when something
    interesting happens rather than adding another row to a database.
</p>

<h3>
    So, What Is This?
</h3>

<p>
    This site is becoming the public-facing archive for those
    projects.
</p>

<p>
    The collection data can stay in Notion, while this site can
    document the reasoning and stories behind it.
</p>

<p>
    Think of it as a scrapbook, research notebook, and collection
    archive that happen to live on the internet.
</p>
